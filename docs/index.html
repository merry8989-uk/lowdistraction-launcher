Android launcher · prototype

A launcher with nothing to do.

Low Distraction replaces your home screen with a plain list of app names. No icons. No wallpaper. No widgets, folders, dock or feed. Nothing to browse, nothing to fiddle with — and a short pause before any app opens, so the tap you make is the tap you meant.

No internet permission No trackers, no ads, no analytics Free & open source · AGPL-3.0 62 themes min Android 8.0
01 — The idea

The home screen should not be a place you enjoy

Most launchers are designed to be looked at. This one is designed to be left.

Every modern home screen is an invitation: a wallpaper to admire, icons to arrange, widgets to check, a search bar that answers anything, a news feed that never ends. That is not an accident — it is the product. A launcher is the first thing you see when you unlock a phone, dozens of times a day.

Low Distraction takes all of that away. What is left is a text list, a search box you have to actually type into, and one deliberate moment of friction before an app opens. It is a small, unglamorous tool with a single opinion: the phone should be boring on purpose.

02 — How it works

Four moments, and then you're done

This is the whole interaction model. There is no third screen, no drawer, no hidden gesture to learn.

STEP ONE

You set it as your Home app

Install, then Settings → Set as default launcher. The app opens Android's own Home-app picker — it never pretends to take over by itself. Pick Low Distraction, press Home, and the text list is your home screen.

A nine-step first-run guide walks you through it once: settings access, the bubble, the wheel and its chime, themes, the search box, permissions, files, and the default-launcher step. Replayable any time from Settings → How to use.

STEP TWO

You see names, not icons

Your apps appear as plain text, in a single column, in the theme's typeface. That's it. The default is name only — no icons at all — though you can switch to icon-only or icon+name in Appearance if you want.

Nothing is ever pinned, sorted or arranged for you. There is no "recommended", no "frequently used", no badge counts. Up to eight (or however many you choose) apps can sit in a small grid at the top — but only the ones you put there.

STEP THREE

You tap an app — and hit a pause

Instead of launching, the screen asks one question: “Do you really need this right now?” A number counts down. Tap anywhere to cancel — the whole screen is the cancel button. If you let it run out, the app opens.

The length is yours: anywhere from 1 second to 2 minutes. A soft tick sounds each second, and the last three are a little brighter so you can hear it running out. The tick has its own on/off switch, and the whole pause can be switched off too.

STEP FOUR

Or you reach for the bubble

A small assistive-touch circle floats over everything. Drag it anywhere; tap it and a radial wheel opens with your pinned apps around it. One thumb, one gesture, no hunting through a list.

It hides itself over 96 payment and government apps — UPI, wallets, banks, brokers, insurers, state and central government portals — recognised by package, and by name for anything it does not already know. It also pauses during gestures, so it never fights your navigation.

03 — Every feature

What is actually in the box

There are no paid tiers, no Pro unlock, no ads and no "coming soon". Everything here is in the build you can download today.

The home screen

Made of words

A single column of app names, in the theme's typeface, with nothing to tap but the app itself. Three display modes if you want icons after all: name only (the default), icon only, or icon + name.

Friction

The pause

One question before an app opens, with a countdown you set — 1 to 120 seconds. Tap anywhere on the screen to cancel. A soft tick sounds each second; the last three are brighter. Tick and pause each have their own switch.

Reach

The bubble & the wheel

An assistive-touch circle that floats over everything and drags anywhere. Tap it for a radial wheel of your pinned apps, plus four quick actions: Torch, Volume, DND and Break. Sixteen selectable wheel sounds, all synthesised in-app.

Privacy

It gets out of the way of money

The bubble hides itself over 96 built-in payment, banking, brokerage, insurance and government packages, and over any app whose name looks financial. Add your own, or exempt one that was caught by mistake.

Finding things

Search, if you ask for it

The search box filters your apps as you type. Five more sources can be switched on individually — Settings, Contacts, Calendar, Files and Dev.Check — each off by default, each asking for its own permission, each removable again from the same screen.

Search

Files, by name

Turn on file search and the launcher walks your storage directly to find a file by name, then opens it with the right app. Nothing is uploaded and nothing is indexed anywhere but on the phone.

Layout

Pinned apps, your grid

A small grid at the top for the handful of things you reach for constantly. You choose its shape — 1 to 10 rows by 1 to 4 columns, three by two by default. Nothing is ever pinned for you.

Order

Seventeen ways to sort

Alphabetical either way, newest or oldest installed, recently updated, most or least opened, most or least time spent, largest or smallest, shortest or longest name — and more. Each order can be switched off individually. Four need usage access and fall back to name order with a note if you decline.

Looks

62 themes, plus your own

Thirty-one dark, thirty light, plus Graphite — all generated from a hue and a strength, so they feel like one family. Or build your own from four colours with an HSV picker. Icon size, shape and outline; text size, weight and letter-spacing; and the slow drifting glow can be turned off.

Quiet corners

Hidden apps

Send an app off the list without uninstalling it. It stays openable from Settings → Hidden apps, and comes straight back when you unhide it.

Gestures

Double-tap to lock

Double-tap the header or an empty part of the list and the screen locks — no reaching for the power button. Long-press anywhere for settings.

Built in

Dev.Check

A small device dashboard that ships inside the launcher: hardware, system, battery, network, installed apps, camera, and live sensor readouts that update as you hold the phone. No ads, no upsell.

And one more

Tea Mode — a five-minute break

One tap on the wheel puts the phone down for you: a full-screen break timer that holds the phone quiet for five minutes. The point is not the timer. The point is that stepping away should be as easy as one tap, since the whole launcher is built to make stepping away easier.

Nothing is ever done for you. No app is auto-pinned, auto-sorted or auto-hidden, no source is enabled by default, and no permission is requested until you turn on the feature that needs it.
04 — The screens

The whole app, drawn in HTML

These are not photographs. Because the launcher is text, its screens can be rebuilt exactly in CSS — so they stay sharp at any size, and the drifting glow moves the way it does on the phone. Toggle your system theme to see both a dark and a light theme.

05 — Privacy & permissions

It cannot phone home, because it cannot phone

The source manifest declares android.permission.INTERNET with tools:node="remove", which strips it out of the final app no matter what a dependency tries to add. A check in the Gradle build fails the build outright if that guard is ever deleted. The released APK contains no internet permission at all — not a policy, a fact you can verify on the file.

No trackers, no analytics, no crash reporting, no ads, no accounts. There is nowhere for data to go, so nothing is collected in the first place.

07 — Questions

Things people ask

Is this just a digital-wellbeing app in disguise?

No. It does not count your usage, score you, or send you a weekly report — it has no internet to send it with. The only friction it adds is the pause you configure yourself, and it makes no judgement about how much you use your phone.

Can I turn the pause off?

Yes, with one switch. The timer, the tick and the pause itself are three separate controls, so you can keep the ticking timer with a sound off, or a silent timer, or no timer at all.

Why is the file search permission so broad?

Finding a file by name anywhere in storage is only possible with all-files access on modern Android. It is off by default and only requested when you switch file search on, and you can revoke it from the same screen. If you would rather not grant it, the launcher keeps a manual folder-picker fallback.

Does the bubble need accessibility access?

Only for the bubble, and only for the parts that read taps and gestures. The launcher works fully without it — the bubble simply will not appear. Nothing is logged, stored or transmitted by the accessibility service.

Will my home screen survive a reboot?

Yes — as long as it is set as the default Home app in Android's own settings. The launcher opens that system picker for you rather than asking for the role itself.

Where do I report a bug?

On the repository, using the issue template. The source is AGPL-3.0, so you are also free to build it yourself — the build is plain Gradle, and CI publishes the exact APK for every tag.
